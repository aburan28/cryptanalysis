# Cold, full-rank one-target controls at n37, n41, n53

Two frozen cells completed and one timed out. At n37 and n41, the pinned four-summand exact-support producer built a fresh factor base and full-rank factor-log table, then recovered one new public target. A separate signed-Frobenius rho producer solved that identical point. The independent pure-Python verifier replayed **all 225 emitted group relations**, their modular row equations and rank histories, and both target scalars. It also checked all factor-base points on the curve, every signed-Frobenius orbit, a subgroup-order annihilation for every orbit representative, primality of each subgroup order, and the curve order from the binary Koblitz trace recurrence. The n53 direct arm timed out at its frozen 180-second limit **before emitting a target**; it has no rho arm or completed candidate ID. No seed was replaced. The CPU host was not isolated, so all times below are exploratory and controlled speedup remains `null`.

The executed runner is fixed by preregistration commit `d4e67c34`; its later environment guard applies only to future replays. The [protocol](PROTOCOL.md) was committed and opened as [PR #353](https://github.com/aburan28/cryptanalysis/pull/353) before target generation. It pins the producer at `crypto` commit `c72806c87f2f480cca90bf837152811276fad3a1`, its exact source and lockfile hashes, the three seeds, one precomputation fixture plus one primary target, 16 GiB RSS gate, run order, and stop limits. Rebuilt release binaries matched the hashes on the earlier n41/n53 online receipts byte-for-byte. The [preflight note](PREFLIGHT.md) preserves a corrected transcription error that stopped before any target was generated.

The preparation collectors used 29,360 support probes for 45 verified rows at n37 and 1,430,512 probes for 178 verified rows at n41, with zero duplicate or scalar-multiple rows. The one-target steps used 464 and 64 support probes respectively. These are probes inside a structured pair-index search, not independent Bernoulli natural-query trials, so they do not estimate a general PDP hit probability. One run per field provides no empirical uncertainty interval for timing or yield; the full relation receipts and censored n53 attempt remain in the raw records.

The published producer timer **double-counts final solving and solution validation**. Its `collection_ms` clock begins before relation collection and stops *after* those two subphases; `target_online_wall_ms` then adds them again. The raw n37 online total is 9.219 ms and repeats 3.187 ms. The raw n41 total is 6.544 ms and repeats 1.930 ms. The corrected exclusive interval is `fixture_setup_ms + collection_ms + reference_validation_ms`. Its five phases are query; PDP and internal checks excluding final solve/replay; external relation check; zero recursive descent; and final solve/replay. [The verifier](verify.py) checks that these phases sum exactly and retains both raw and corrected values. This correction changes timing accounting, not any group result. The producer needs its own patch before new comparisons can use its published online fields directly.

The n37 cold interval consists of 5.729 ms curve setup, 43.666 ms base/index setup, 501.202 ms full-rank log preparation and checks, and 6.032 ms target work. At n41 the corresponding costs are 82.151, 25,931.827, 2,289.657 and 4.614 ms. The n41 exact-support index alone took 25,902.961 ms, about 91.5% of its corrected cold interval. These are single-run stage diagnostics on a contended host. Rho's intervals are its first target-dependent walk step through scalar replay; the IC cold interval excludes process launch and scalar-derived fixture point construction. Full process walls are retained separately in the resource receipts. Do not divide the paired times into a promoted speedup without the required isolated-host receipt.

The first-run driver inherited its ambient environment and recorded only its explicit `KIC_*` overrides; it did not snapshot every absent key. The candidate manifests therefore use **effective** algorithm flags reconstructed from the producer receipts (including batch corpus, surplus, rank mode and exact Bloom-filter allocation). The runner now fails before target generation if unexpected ambient `KIC_*` variables are set, closing that provenance gap for future replays. This retrospective correction does not change the archived first-run outputs or make their CPU timings controlled.

The successful cells have immutable [candidate, workload, and run manifests](identities/index.json) with curve IDs, full point-list digests, exact method/source and factor-base seeds. The workload IDs are `ea0af18fc73e` (n37) and `b4ae0cf0dac4` (n41). The n53 timeout is retained as a censored attempt; it cannot be promoted to an `IC1` candidate or assigned a workload ID because no exact base or public target was emitted. Raw compressed JSONL, stderr, resource receipts and the full [independent replay](runs/verification.json) are under `runs/`; the verifier checks the uncompressed SHA-256 of each archive against its receipt.

**Decision.** For the cold one-target ladder, stop treating the explicit signed-expanded pair index as a likely n53 winner based on its warm online numbers. Reproduce n53 with the compact-orbit support construction, a streaming setup receipt and corrected exclusive timers, keeping the same point and resource contract. The current n53 timeout is a bounded failure on this host, not an algorithmic impossibility: an earlier source-identical binary completed on a different run. For the ECC2K-130 objective, these toy-curve controls do not supply n131 PDP yield, rank, target descent, or an end-to-end attack. Continue the frozen equal-size W24/degree-263 ordinary-query gate independently; do not transfer this toy online crossover to n131.
