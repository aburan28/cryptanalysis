# A sparse Eisenstein radix family

The family `πₖ=(2ᵏ−1)+2ᵏτ`, with `τ²=3τ−3`, keeps the short point action `πₖP=[2ᵏ](P+τP)−P` while changing the digit-table size and recoding length. Its exact norm is `Nₖ=7·2²ᵏ−5·2ᵏ+1`, and the cyclic quotient has the explicit residue `τ↦4−7·2ᵏ` modulo `Nₖ`. These identities give a concrete design space around the measured `k=7` radix, including smaller tables that may improve one-scalar cold cost.

## Algebra and proof map

For an arbitrary integer `t`, define `πₜ=(t−1)+tτ`. Multiplication on an integer pair is

`πₜ(u,v)=((t−1)u−3tv, tu+(4t−1)v)`.

The [pinned Lean 4.34.1 module](SparseRadixFamily.lean) checks seven results: `family_shortcut` proves the `[t](1+τ)−1` action; `family_norm` proves `N(πₜ)=7t²−5t+1`; `family_residue` proves `(t−1)+t(4−7t)=−N(πₜ)`; `family_residue_polynomial` proves the quotient root satisfies `s²−3s+3=7N(πₜ)` for `s=4−7t`; and `family_norm_scaling` proves `N(πₜz)=N(πₜ)N(z)` for every integer pair `z`. The sixth theorem, `radix64_terminal_from_norm_step`, proves that an initial pair norm at most `7·2²⁶⁰` and the explicit 17-step recurrence `100·28353·qᵢ₊₁ ≤ 101·qᵢ+10100·9451` imply `q₁₇<5000`. The seventh theorem, `radix32_terminal_from_norm_step`, proves that the same initial bound and `100·7009·qᵢ₊₁ ≤ 101·qᵢ+10100·2284` for 20 steps imply `q₂₀<5000`. These recurrences and uniform digit caps are explicit hypotheses; their bridge to the finite selectors remains open. The [fresh checker](check_sparse_radix_family_lean.py) and [receipt](sparse-radix-family-lean-check.json) retain the source hashes, successful builds, and `#print axioms` for each declaration. The proofs use the standard Lean foundations `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

## Concrete secp256k1 subgroup action

The pinned Lean 4.34.1 [Secp256k1ScalarRadix.lean](Secp256k1ScalarRadix.lean) fixes the secp256k1 subgroup order `115792089237316195423570985008687907852837564279074904382605163141518161494337` and the recoder's eigenvalue `λτ=78074008874160198520644763525212887401909906723592317393988542598630163514320`. It checks `λτ²−3λτ+3≡0 (mod r)`, addition of pair scalar values, multiplication by `τ`, and the action of every `(t−1)+tτ` modulo `r`. It also transfers an exact checkpoint block reconstruction to the subgroup scalar. The [fresh checker](check_secp256k1_scalar_radix_lean.py) and [receipt](secp256k1-scalar-radix-lean-check.json) retain source hashes, successful builds, and `#print axioms` for all five declarations, with only standard Lean foundations.

An independent [affine generator replay](check_secp256k1_tau_generator.py) uses the hash-pinned executed source and binary from the checkpointed radix-32 panel. Its [receipt](secp256k1-tau-generator-check.json) checks that the native projective `τ` formula normalized at `G` equals both `G−ωG`, where `ω(x,y)=(βx,y)`, and `[λτ]G`; it also checks the curve equation, `β²+β+1≡0 (mod p)`, the subgroup order, and the eigenvalue polynomial.

The additional pinned Lean [Secp256k1TauFormula.lean](Secp256k1TauFormula.lean) proves two polynomial cross-product identities and their reductions modulo the exact secp256k1 field prime. For `w=x−βx`, `rₓ=4y²−3x³`, and `rᵧ=(3x³−2rₓ)y`, the modular results identify `rₓ` with the x numerator and `rᵧ` with the y numerator of the affine chord calculation for `P−ωP`. The [fresh checker](check_secp256k1_tau_formula_lean.py) and [receipt](secp256k1-tau-formula-lean-check.json) retain source hashes, successful builds, and `#print axioms` for all four declarations. The remaining curve-wide bridge is to prove denominator nonvanishing for every relevant nonidentity point, connect the chord equations to the elliptic-curve group law, and refine the native `U256` arithmetic and point representation to these modular formulas. The subgroup scalar theorem does not assume those curve-coordinate statements.
