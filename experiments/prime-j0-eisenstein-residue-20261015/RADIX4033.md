# Norm-4033 Eisenstein radix for scalar-local multiplication

The scalar-local path can use `π = 64+ω = 65−τ`, whose hexagonal norm is **4033**. Its Horner action is six doublings, one order-three x rotation, and one projective addition. The [hash-guarded overlay](native_sparse_radix4033_overlay.py) also changes the nearest-digit selector, exact quotient matrix, and step count from 20 to 22. The quotient is cyclic with `τ ≡ 65 (mod 4033)`; primality of the norm is not required for the recorded division and replay. The [Lean module](Radix4033.lean) checks the norm, adjugate identities, exact quotient under the selector congruence, and rotation identity. Its [fresh pinned receipt](radix4033-lean-check.json) records five axiom audits.

The follow-on [Lean bounds module](Radix4033Bounds.lean) proves that a rounded candidate for every signed residue has norm below 4033. Any selector that chooses a no-larger norm inherits that bound. It then proves the quotient norm recurrence and shows that **22 exact selected steps** from the existing secp256k1 four-corner initial envelope reach norm at most **101**, placing both terminal coordinates strictly inside the native radius-100 table. The [fresh pinned receipt](radix4033-bounds-lean-check.json) records nine additional axiom audits with only standard Lean foundations. The final theorem states its selector-minimum, exact-quotient, and initial lattice hypotheses explicitly.

The [source-bound selector audit](audit_radix4033_selector.py) checks the exact native selector and quotient snippets in the archived source, then compares all **2,017** nonnegative signed residues against an independent exhaustive lattice minimum. Negation covers the other 2,016 residue classes. All 4,033 classes satisfy the quotient congruence; the maximum selected-digit norm is **1,323** and coordinate magnitudes are at most **63** and **42**. The [receipt](radix4033-selector-audit.json) retains source and provenance hashes. Formal refinement of the executed signed-limb arithmetic, selector loop, and curve group operations remains open.

The [finite-selector Lean model](Radix4033Selector.lean) now closes the mathematical search step: it proves the native five-by-five window contains the rounded witness, proves a least-norm fold returns a digit no worse than that witness, and proves congruence and exact quotient construction. Its signed branch covers every residue modulo 4033. The final `total_selected_step` theorem supplies both a digit of norm below 4033 and an exact next quotient for every integer-pair state, without a selector-minimum hypothesis. A [fresh pinned receipt](radix4033-selector-lean-check.json) records twelve additional axiom audits. The model retains a previous candidate on equal norms; Rust uses further deterministic tie breakers. The source-bound exhaustive audit checks the actual tie choices, while a Lean refinement of that executed ordering and signed-limb implementation remains open.

## Paired Tier 1 measurements

The committed [one-scalar](tier1-radix4033-1-cpu40-manifest.json) and [128-point](tier1-radix4033-128-cpu40-manifest.json) plans compare norm 4033 with the norm-7009 rotated path on identical inputs, CPU 40, and 16 alternating repeats with adjacent A/A controls. Their [rules](tier1-radix4033-1-cpu40-rules.json) and [rules](tier1-radix4033-128-cpu40-rules.json) freeze the `[0.90,1.11]` noise gate and paired interval criterion. The Rust interval includes recoding, graph digit synthesis, and Horner evaluation; process launch, fixture loading, and external replay are outside it.

| Workload | Norm-7009 mean | Norm-4033 mean | Qualified paired reference/candidate median | Paired bootstrap 95% interval |

| One scalar | 0.542 ms | 0.471 ms | 1.140× | [1.130, 1.144] |

The initial [128-point correctness pilot](tier1-radix4033-128-pilot-cpu40-launch.json) and [raw record](radix4033-128-pilot-raw.tar.gz) are retained separately.

## Fixed-base secp256k1 library comparison

The same 128 frozen scalars now have an [OpenSSL `EC_POINT_mul` baseline](openssl_secp256k1_baseline.c) with the exact [scalar and point fixture](openssl-secp256k1-first128-fixture.txt). The repository's generic `rho.c` group API does not implement secp256k1 field arithmetic, so this comparison uses the pod's OpenSSL 3.0.13 library as an established secp256k1 arithmetic reference. The OpenSSL interval includes multiplication and affine `x:y` serialization. It excludes fixture parsing, curve setup, one warm multiplication, and process launch. The norm-4033 interval includes scalar recoding, graph digit synthesis, Horner evaluation, and affine output. OpenSSL's excluded warm multiplication averaged 1.114 ms and 1.119 ms across the respective panels; its value is retained in the audits.

Both [one-scalar](tier1-radix4033-vs-openssl-1-cpu40-manifest.json) and [128-point](tier1-radix4033-vs-openssl-128-cpu40-manifest.json) plans used CPU 40 on RunPod allocation `thyvmdqmzsz5ik`, 16 alternating repeats, identical points, and OpenSSL A/A cases before and after each comparison. The [one-scalar rules](tier1-radix4033-vs-openssl-1-cpu40-rules.json) and [128-point rules](tier1-radix4033-vs-openssl-128-cpu40-rules.json) froze binary, source, fixture, linked library hashes, the `[0.90,1.11]` A/A gate, and the paired interval criterion before dispatch.

| Workload | OpenSSL mean | Norm-4033 mean | Qualified paired OpenSSL/norm-4033 median | Paired bootstrap 95% interval |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| One scalar | 0.220 ms | 0.474 ms | 0.460× | [0.458, 0.470] |
| 128 points | 27.653 ms | 38.792 ms | 0.714× | [0.712, 0.717] |

The [independent auditor](analyze_tier1_radix4033_vs_openssl.py) checked all source, binary, fixture, and `libcrypto` hashes against the [native provenance](openssl-secp256k1-native-provenance.tar.gz), independently replayed the measured points, checked every command/stdout/stderr and pair, and passed all adjacent A/A controls. Each panel completed **96 valid arms and 48 valid pairs**. The [one-scalar launch](tier1-radix4033-vs-openssl-1-cpu40-launch.json), [audit](tier1-radix4033-vs-openssl-1-cpu40-audit.json), and [raw archive](radix4033-vs-openssl-1-raw.tar.gz) identify job `efdcff6ed47646598f58aed0e059bcf5`; the [128-point launch](tier1-radix4033-vs-openssl-128-cpu40-launch.json), [audit](tier1-radix4033-vs-openssl-128-cpu40-audit.json), and [raw archive](radix4033-vs-openssl-128-raw.tar.gz) identify job `71ebf8c5348a487a9a505933bb892b93`.

The norm-4033 selector and Horner step improve the preceding Eisenstein implementation, while this library comparison identifies the remaining implementation gap. The next engineering experiment should keep the proven radix/selector and test its point operations with a conventional secp256k1 field backend and reusable fixed-base table under the same scalar-local boundary. Integration into the rho path should follow a matched walk-step or restart benchmark on its actual curve and input law.
