\begin{abstract}
On a Koblitz curve over $\FF_{2^n}$ the Frobenius endomorphism $\tau$
and negation generate a group $\Gamma$ of order $2n$ acting on the
prime-order subgroup, and an index calculus with a $\Gamma$-invariant
factor base needs $2n$ times fewer unknowns. That reduction is known
(Galbraith, Granger, Merz and Petit, SAC~2020). What it costs to
realize is not: every decomposed point has to be mapped to its orbit
representative together with its sign and Frobenius phase
$k$, the tables the pipeline probes have to be keyed by
representatives, and the phase has to survive from the decomposition
stage, which sees only $x$-coordinates, to the relation matrix, which
needs $\lambda^k$. This paper treats that as an algorithmic problem.
We define the signed-Frobenius representation $P=\epsilon\,\tau^{-k}\hat P$
and prove that orbits in the prime subgroup are always full when $n$
is prime, so the only exceptional words are $x\in\FF_2$. We give an
exact shortcut for the normal-basis least-rotation canonical form,
built on the standard fact that the least rotation of a binary necklace
begins at a longest run of zeros, prove it bit-identical to the exhaustive
scan, and measure it at $4.78$ rotations per $83$-bit word against
$82$. We describe phase-carrying layouts: an $18$-byte root record
with a $7$-bit phase, a $2.5\times$ smaller index than an open-addressed
table; an orbit tag that makes summand recovery in a folded pair table
$O(n)$ instead of $O(|F|)$; and the rule that canonical keys must never
be indexed by their leading bits. We then report what survives complete
relation collection, with every logarithm verified: a $\Gamma$-orbit
Gr\"obner base solves a complete ECDLP at $n=19$ in $6.85\times$ less
CPU than a plain base; normal-basis canonicalization makes the
signed-Frobenius Pollard rho reference $4.1\times$ cheaper in retired
instructions, which reverses an apparent index-calculus win; and
orbit folding of the pair table divides stored entries by $2n$ while
moving total operations by $+1.2\%$ at one width and $-12\%$ at
another. Nothing here is faster than Pollard rho: at every degree
priced, $n\le83$, the phase-aware index calculus is $1.3$--$300\times$
the matched rho. The contribution is the representation, its proofs,
its measured costs, and the discipline that keeps a constant-factor
gain in one stage from being reported as an attack.
\end{abstract}
